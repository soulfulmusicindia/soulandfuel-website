Almost every enquiry asks how many images come with the shoot. Fair question. It also decides the least.

Look at where the pictures are going. A home page holds about six. A room page on a hotel site holds five. A designer showing a project to a new client gets through maybe ten before the conversation moves on.

So the count is not the thing to negotiate. The mix is.

A set that carries a property has a wide frame that shows proportion and how the rooms sit together.

It has a picture taken at standing height, of the space being used rather than presented, so a guest can put themselves in it.

And it has one close frame of the thing somebody argued about for a month. The brass, the stone floor, the joinery around the window.

Most folders are heavy on the first kind and thin on the other two.

Shooting one room eight ways does not give you eight pictures. It gives you one, and seven that look like it. Two rooms done properly are worth more than one room done eight times.

For a hotel, one strong frame per room type does more for a booking page than thirty bedroom photos that all look alike.

For an architect the deck and the website want different pictures. Tell me which one the shoot is feeding and the list changes before I arrive.

Designers, when the photos came back last time, how many of them did you put anywhere?
