# Soul & Fuel LinkedIn Brief

Standing brief for the twice weekly LinkedIn post (Sunday and Wednesday) for Soul & Fuel Media, Mahesh's interior, architecture, real estate and hospitality photography studio in Bengaluru. Website: soulandfuel.com. This repo is the website.

Goal of every post: make Mahesh look like the person who obviously knows this craft, and bring in hotel, resort, architect, designer and developer clients.

## Step 1. Check what has already been used

Read `linkedin-drafts/USED-TOPICS.md`. Read the two most recent dated files in `linkedin-drafts/`. Never repeat a topic or an image that appears there.

If `USED-TOPICS.md` does not exist, create it and seed it with these three topics, which have already been drafted:

- Mixed colour temperature, and glossy floors bouncing colour onto white walls
- Getting rooms ready before shoot day, and what an unready room costs
- Why one interior shoot costs 20,000 and another costs 2 lakh

## Step 2. Pick a topic

Posts are never built around a named project or client. Never name a property or a client in the copy. The picture stops the scroll. The words teach something.

Rotate across these buckets:

1. **Technique.** Small bathrooms with nowhere to stand back. Mirrors and glass bouncing the crew into frame. Ceiling fans. Windows blowing out. Vertical lines going crooked. Bad downlights.
2. **Pain points.** Rooms not ready. Furniture arriving late. Site still half built. Being given two hours for a job that needs six.
3. **Good practice.** Recce before the shoot. Shot lists. Shooting rooms in the order the sun moves. Asking for a floor plan in advance. What is in the styling kit.
4. **Advice to clients.** What to prep before the photographer arrives. How many images a property actually needs. Why the cheapest quote costs more later. Why waiting for the landscaping to mature means you never shoot at all.
5. **Myth busting.** Over edited HDR. Fake blue skies. Neon green grass. Wide angle lenses lying about room size.
6. **Business value.** What good images do for direct bookings, a listing, or an architect's next pitch. Useful, never a sales pitch.

Lean toward buckets 1, 2 and 4. They get the most engagement from his audience.

## Step 3. Pick images

Images come from `images/projects/<slug>/` in this repo. Actually open and look at the candidates before choosing. Do not pick on filename alone.

- Pick images that illustrate the topic. Post about small spaces, pick a tight bathroom. Post about window light, pick a room with one strong window.
- Strong scroll stoppers: drone frames, dramatic ceilings, pools, night exteriors with warm windows, rich materials like red oxide floors or dark wood.
- Suggest one to three images by exact path, and say why each was picked.
- If the post has a numbered list, suggest a carousel of four or five.

## Step 4. Writing rules. These override everything.

**Simple English.** Write the way a person types, not the way a brand writes. Short common words. No literary phrasing, no wordplay, no magazine copy. Read it back and ask whether Mahesh would say that sentence out loud to a client.

**No dashes and no hyphens.** Never use an em dash, an en dash, or a hyphen, including in compound words. Write "behind the scenes" not the hyphenated form. Write "two hour shoot", "high end", "west facing". Use a full stop, comma or brackets instead. Check the finished text character by character.

**Never use these words and phrases:** delve, at its core, elevate, unlock, seamless, game changer, robust, journey as a metaphor, tapestry, realm, underscore, testament to, curated, unleash, empower, holistic, synergy, quietly, shift, matters, shape, land, actually as filler, "real" as an intensifier, "earn" attached to abstractions, "the work", compound, signal, "In today's fast paced world", "at the end of the day", "the truth is", "here's the thing", "I'm excited to share", "I'm thrilled to announce", "Let's talk about". Also cut empty intensifiers: truly, incredibly, amazing, stunning, incredible.

**Never use these sentence shapes:** "It's not X, it's Y" and all its variants. Groups of three with matched rhythm. Repeating the same phrase in the opening and the closing line. Every sentence being the same length. Resolving every thought neatly.

Instead: concrete details over adjectives, a real number, a real time of day, a real thing that went wrong. Vary line lengths hard. Some lines can be four words.

## Step 5. Facts about Mahesh. Do not contradict these and do not invent new ones.

- **He shoots with all artificial lights switched off. Natural light only.** Ceiling lights, lamps and cove lighting all go off. Never describe him blending an ambient frame with a lamps on frame. Never call his work flambient. His answer to mixed colour temperature is to remove the artificial source and wait for better daylight.
- His work has been published in Elle Decor, India Today Homes, Architect & Interiors India, Good Homes India and The New Indian Express. Mention at most one, only where it fits the point. Never list them.
- He shoots across India, residential, hospitality and studio.

If a post needs a process detail not listed here, do not guess. Write the post without it, and flag the gap as a question for Mahesh.

## Step 6. Structure

1. **Hook**, one or two lines. This is all most people read. Plain, specific, slightly surprising. A number works. A confession works. A common mistake works.
2. **Body**, three to six short paragraphs. One idea each.
3. **The turn.** Why this affects a hotel owner or an architect about to book a shoot.
4. **Soft close.** A question that invites replies beats a pitch. Keep any DM ask light and occasional.
5. **Hashtags**, three to five, specific. Examples: #InteriorPhotography #ArchitecturalPhotography #HospitalityDesign #RealEstatePhotography

Short lines, lots of white space, almost no emoji. The brand is premium and calm, not hustle culture.

## Step 7. Output

Write `linkedin-drafts/YYYY-MM-DD.md` containing, in this order: the topic bucket, the hook on its own line, the full post text ready to paste, a second hook option, the image picks with exact paths and reasons, and a short list of anything you guessed that Mahesh should check before posting.

Append one line to `USED-TOPICS.md` recording the date, the topic and the images used.

Commit both files and push to master. Change nothing else in the repo.

Then print the complete draft in your final message so Mahesh can read it without opening a file.
